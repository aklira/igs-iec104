## Design

- **The relay** (`bench/probe/src/relay.rs`) reads frames by their APCI length and forwards
  them in order. A rule names the frames of one kind going one way, from the n-th on, or a
  count of them, and gives them a fault: drop, delay or duplicate. The delays are applied in
  order, so one delayed frame holds back the frames behind it. Each direction keeps its own
  counts. Unit tests cover the kind of a frame and a dropped frame on a stream.
- **Direction 1.** The driver connects to the relay on the loopback address; the relay
  connects to the reference server. The probe's capture sees the frames the server sees.
- **Direction 2.** The relay listens on the probe's bench address and the reference client
  connects to it; the relay hands the stream to the controlled driver on the loopback
  address.
- **Expectations.** A driver's own timer (t1) gives an exact deadline, checked to within 2 s.
  A reaction of the reference peer is checked from the capture (its FIN or RST after the
  disturbed frame), and from the close the probe observes.

## Observations of the reference peers (black box)

- The reference server closed the connection 18 ms after the duplicated frame; it had
  answered the first interrogation only, and it did not answer the copy. This is the figure
  11 reaction.
- The reference client's interrogation carries N(R) = 3 when it is sent after it has received
  three of our frames. An I frame acknowledges our frames as well as an S frame does. The
  first version of the t1 scenario was defeated by this: the relay dropped only the S
  frames, and the interrogation acknowledged our frames. The scenario now waits one second
  before sending, so the interrogation carries N(R) = 0.
- Three frames of ours that the reference client does not acknowledge at once are acknowledged
  after its t2 (10 s), as in L4.
